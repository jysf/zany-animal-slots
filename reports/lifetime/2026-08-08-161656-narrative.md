## Part 1 — The app itself

**What it is:** a play-money, mobile-first slot game themed on North American
wildlife, built to prove that game *logic* and *presentation* can stay
cleanly separated (DEC-001) — that thesis is what the whole repo dogfoods.

**How the feature set grew, project by project:**

- **PROJ-001 (Jun 18 → Jul 3, 16 days, 6 stages):** the MVP. Engine (seedable
  RNG, 5×3 grid, 5 fixed paylines, win tiers), reels UI + spin flow, win
  celebration/juice, a full synthesized audio suite (Tone.js) plus an
  accessibility pass (reduced motion, contrast, colorblind cues, touch
  targets) *inside the same stage* — a11y wasn't bolted on later. Shipped
  with security headers, a CI supply-chain gate, and a disclosure policy
  before calling it done.
- **PROJ-002 (Jul 3 → 12, 9 days, 7 stages):** stress-tested the
  engine/presentation split by making the machine itself config-driven
  (DEC-015) and adding three new machines — Arctic, Desert, Ocean — each
  just data, zero engine changes. Also added session stats + a sparkline,
  first-run help/onboarding, and a *gated* analytics posture (Tier 1
  shipped default-OFF; Tier 2 remote sinks explicitly parked pending an
  amendment + go-ahead).
- **PROJ-003 (Jul 23 → 24, 1 day):** meta-progression. Trophy model, trophy
  case, the "trophy-earned" moment, replay, folded into a tabbed record
  sheet. Also walked back the ambient audio loop to quiet-by-default
  (DEC-025) — a rare "remove a feature" spec.
- **PROJ-004 (Jul 24, same day):** the ad-probe — explicitly framed as a
  reversible UX study, not monetization: fake interstitial/popup/banner
  house ads, an ad control panel, a rewarded-ad-for-coins mechanic.
  First-party and offline only, no real ad network or real money.
- **PROJ-005 (Jul 24 → 25):** two more machines — Farm (high-variance, rare
  fat jackpot) and Diner (generous/high-hit-rate) — the fourth and fifth
  proof that config-as-data scales to genuinely different math
  personalities without touching the engine.
- **PROJ-006 (started Jul 29, still active):** cabinet chrome/polish —
  framing, a real machine switcher, naming, header layout, watermark,
  adaptive height. Three stages and 11 specs shipped in a single day
  (Jul 29), but the *project* itself hasn't shipped — it's been sitting
  active for 10 days with no further commits.

**End state:** 6 machines with distinct math (Whimsy/default, Arctic,
Desert, Ocean, Farm, Diner), trophies + replay, session stats, first-run
help, a gated analytics layer, a gated ad-probe, deployed on Cloudflare
(migrated Pages → Workers static assets mid-flight, DEC-008 → DEC-014).

**Loose thread:** an untracked audio-spike investigation
(`docs/audio-spike-2026-07-15.md`, `audio-spike.html`,
`src/ui/audio/_spike/`) diagnoses why the synthesized audio "reads cheap"
(bone-dry Tone.js voices, zero effects nodes anywhere in `src/`, a
repeating-chord ambient bed). It's a recommendation-plus-A/B-demo, not
committed work — nothing ships from it without an explicit go.

## Part 2 — The process, first pass

**Methodology:** a spec-driven template, "claude-only" variant — Claude is
architect and implementer in separate sessions, and each spec file carries
its own full implementation context. Work nests strictly: Repo → Project →
Stage → Spec → Cycle (frame/design/build/verify/ship).

**Decisions as durable memory:** 28 ADRs accumulated across 6 projects, and
they bind forward — e.g. DEC-015 (config-driven machine model) from PROJ-002
is the reason PROJ-005 could add two more machines in a single day with zero
engine changes. That compounding is visible in the velocity: the MVP took 16
days for 6 stages; by July 23–25 the process was shipping entire projects
(trophy case, ad-probe, two new machines) in 1–2 days each.

**Cost discipline:** every build/verify cycle logs real token counts, rolled
up at ship time, CI-gated by `just cost-audit`. Lifetime total: **$108.99 /
12.68M tokens across 102 shipped specs** — roughly $1.07/spec average.

**Guardrails that held:** two features stayed deliberately gated rather than
shipped opportunistically — Tier 2 analytics sinks (needs a DEC-005
amendment + explicit go) and the ad-probe (offline mocks only, no real ad
network, no real money). Both are still respected as of the last status
check.

## Part 3 — The story

The repo opens on the night of June 18 — project framed, and within the same
evening the architect writes down a rule that will matter a lot later: *Opus
designs, Sonnet builds*. The very first spec (scaffolding) ships slightly
before that rule takes hold and gets built entirely on Opus, at triple the
cost-per-token of everything that follows — a fossil of the one moment
before the convention existed.

From SPEC-002 on, the split holds tight for ten straight days: engine,
reels, win-juice, a full audio suite, an accessibility pass, deploy — 37
specs, all Opus-thinks/Sonnet-builds, all landing at almost exactly the same
$6.60-per-1000-tokens rate. PROJ-001 ships July 3. PROJ-002 opens the same
day and immediately cashes in the investment: the engine gets made
config-driven, and three new machines (Arctic, Desert, Ocean) get bolted on
as pure data, no engine touched.

Then two small tells appear. On July 10, an "audio overhaul" stage gets
framed, then un-scoped and parked as a future candidate within the same
day — first sign of a thread that's going to keep resurfacing without ever
getting picked up. Same day, a stage on gated analytics gets framed off in
an isolated git worktree, which then just sits there, abandoned, never
merged back or cleaned up (it's still on disk today, frozen at July 10).

July 12 is the first real fracture. Ten small visual fixes ship same-day —
header overflow, sheet clipping, icon sizing — and for the first time the
architect stops handing the work to a Sonnet subagent and just fixes it
directly on the main loop, because, as the spec notes put it, "lint/unit
tests do not catch header overflow or a sheet clipping its own close
button." Cost-per-token triples for that batch. Nobody flags it, because the
fixes are small and the dollar totals stay tiny.

Then an 11-day silence. Inside it, on July 15, someone runs a real
investigation into why the game's audio "reads cheap" — diagnoses it
precisely (zero effects nodes anywhere in the codebase, a repeating-chord
ambient bed) and produces a recommendation plus a runnable A/B demo. And
then nothing happens. It's still sitting there, untracked, over three weeks
later.

July 23: Trophy Case opens, and the Opus/Sonnet split briefly returns for
the mechanical work (the trophy schema). But the moment the work turns
emotional and animated — the trophy-earned moment, the replay — the split
erodes again, and by the "quiet by default" spec on July 24 it's gone. It
never comes back. From that spec through the end of every subsequent
project — the rest of Trophy Case, the entire ad-probe, both new machines,
and all of Cabinet Polish — every single spec is Opus end to end. Sonnet
doesn't appear in the cost record again.

July 24 alone ships Trophy Case's polish stage *and* the entire seven-spec
ad-probe project. July 24–25 adds two more machines. July 29 closes three
Cabinet Polish stages — 11 real specs (22 archived files, because this is
also the first point a paired "-timeline" companion file shows up per
spec) — all same-day, all Opus-solo.

Then: ten days of total silence, through today. The project record still
says PROJ-006 is active. The audio-spike files are still untracked.

## Part 4 — What the data actually reveals about process

**The cost curve isn't a price change — it's the architect quietly giving up
on delegation, and it never came back.** Checking the `agents:` /
`cost.sessions[].model` fields directly, not just the rollup dollars:

| Era | Specs | Avg tokens/spec | Avg $/spec | $ per 1k tok |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Opus designs, Sonnet builds (SPEC-002–062) | 61 | 155k | $1.02 | $6.62 |
| B — first defections, visual fixes only (063–080) | 18 | 97k | $0.95 | $9.81 |
| C — Opus solo, no delegation at all (081–103) | 22 | 59k | $1.18 | $20.00 |

The headline the raw report tempts you toward — "later specs got cheap,
$0.20–$0.60 each!" — is backwards. Those late specs are *small in scope*
(single CSS fixes, config-only machine adds), not efficient: per token they
cost **3x** what the original convention was built to avoid. What kept the
dollar total flat across all three eras ($1.02 → $0.95 → $1.18) wasn't the
rate holding — it was scope shrinking fast enough to absorb a tripling
price. That's a real trade that got made, one spec at a time, and as far as
the record shows nobody ever decided it on purpose — it drifted, starting
from "test suites can't catch visual bugs" and ending with routine
config-only work (the Farm machine, a single audio-mute spec) also going
Opus-solo with no attempt to hand it back.

**One piece of scope discipline worth calling out as a positive, not a
gap:** SPEC-094 (a machine-picker sheet) was explicitly reserved,
deliberately deferred, and the deferral reasoning — plus its own revisit
trigger ("if the roster grows past ~8 machines") — is written directly into
the STAGE-020 doc and cross-referenced by three later specs. That's a clean
way to shelve scope without losing the reasoning, in contrast to the audio
thread, which got parked with no equivalent trigger and has just been
drifting for a month.

**Two abandoned worktrees are lying on disk** (`stage011`, frozen July 10;
`fervent-morse-e0a1c8`, frozen July 12) — physical evidence the isolation
pattern got used twice for sensitive/visual work, then dropped rather than
pruned. Both diff clean against current main — nothing hidden inside either
one, just unpruned residue.

**The live loose end is today's state, not anything in the past:** PROJ-006
has shipped code but hasn't shipped as a project, and the repo's gone quiet
for 10 days with an unresolved audio recommendation sitting in the working
tree. That's the one thread actually asking for a decision right now.
